Evidence

Reflection

  1. What: We organised the work for BrightStart by selecting tasks from our user stories and recording them on our Trello board. We used checklists to break larger tasks into smaller steps and make the work easier to manage.
  2. Why it mattered: The sprint backlog helped us understand what needed to be done and which tasks required attention. It kept our work focused on the main goal of creating an educational website for learners in Grades 1–3.
  3. Learned: I learned that breaking work into smaller tasks makes progress easier to monitor. Trello also helped us identify incomplete tasks instead of assuming that a feature was finished.
  4. Challenge: One challenge was keeping track of tasks when some work was still incomplete. Using Trello checklists helped us identify the remaining steps and see what still needed to be done.
  5. Do differently: In future sprints, I would estimate the time needed for each task more carefully, review progress regularly, and raise delays early so the team can adjust its plan.
  6. Connection: The sprint backlog was developed from the product backlog. It helped us turn prioritised user stories into actionable tasks and provided a way to track the team's progress throughout development.
