Our user stories were based on the goals defined in our Agile Team Charter. BrightStart aims to improve English reading, writing, and comprehension for learners in Grades 1–3 through reading exercises, writing practice, simple quizzes, and progress tracking. We considered the needs of learners, parents, and teachers when identifying the features required for the website. Each user story describes a user's need and the benefit of the feature, helping the team understand what we needed to develop. We then organised the work into smaller tasks and used our Trello Kanban board to track progress through the To Do, In Progress, Testing, and Done stages. This helped us organise responsibilities, monitor our workflow, and work towards the project goals defined in our team charter.

Reflection

  1. What: We identified user needs from our BrightStart project goals and used them to describe the features required for the website. We considered learners, parents, and teachers when creating user stories. We then broke the work into smaller tasks using checklists on Trello cards so that each feature could be managed more easily.
  2. Why it mattered: Thinking from the learner's perspective helped us focus on reading exercises, writing practice, and simple quizzes for learners in Grades 1–3. Considering parents and teachers also highlighted the importance of progress tracking. This helped us keep the website focused on its purpose of improving English literacy.
  3. Learned: I learned that breaking a feature into smaller tasks makes it easier to monitor progress on Trello. For example, a page-development task can involve designing the layout, adding content, and checking that the page works correctly. Each step needs attention before the feature can be considered complete.
  4. Challenge: A challenge was understanding how to divide a larger feature into smaller, manageable tasks. We could approach this by identifying the main steps required to develop the feature and recording them as checklist items on the relevant Trello card.
  5. Do differently: In future projects, I would make acceptance criteria more specific and measurable. For example, I would state what a learner should be able to do on a page and how we would test whether the feature works as expected.
  6. Connection: The user stories helped us identify the features needed for BrightStart and organise them in the product backlog. The backlog could then be prioritised and divided into sprint tasks, connecting users' needs with the team's development work.
