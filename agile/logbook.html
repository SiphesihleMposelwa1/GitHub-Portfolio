Evidence

Reflection

  1. What: As the Agile Lead, I understood the importance of recording our project activities, including planning, team discussions, progress updates, and retrospectives. The logbook helped document our work and keep a record of the activities carried out during the project.
  2. Why it mattered: The logbook helped me understand the importance of accountability because it provided a record of the tasks completed, challenges experienced, and progress made towards our BrightStart project goals.
  3. Learned: I learned that when a task is delayed or a problem affects progress, the team needs to communicate and agree on a solution. Recording blockers makes it easier to identify outstanding work and decide on the next steps.
  4. Challenge: One challenge was maintaining consistent logbook entries while balancing project tasks and development work. I learned that updating the logbook regularly is more effective than trying to remember all the activities later.
  5. Do differently: In future sprints, I would update the logbook after planning sessions and progress discussions. I would also use retrospective feedback to identify improvements, assign actions, and check whether those changes were implemented in the next sprint.
  6. Connection: The logbook connected the sprint backlog with the team's actual progress by recording completed tasks, outstanding work, and challenges. It also helped identify whether our working agreements in the team charter needed to be reviewed or improved.
